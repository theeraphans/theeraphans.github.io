A quotation is not yet an order

Sales teams need to follow up on quotations that have not converted into orders. When there are many open quotations, the difficult part is deciding which ones deserve attention now and giving the team a consistent next step.

I worked on an autonomous sales-recovery workflow at MiSUMi to make that process repeatable. It connects quotation and order data with prioritization rules and the team's follow-up tools.

Find the gap, then decide what to follow up

The workflow compares quotations with order information to identify opportunities that still need follow-up. It considers quotation value, age, and lead-time context rather than treating every open quotation as equally urgent.

Business rules and heuristic inference guide the recovery decision. Airflow schedules the monitoring process, while Power Automate connects its decisions to tasks, notifications, and email workflows. The sales team receives a prioritized opportunity rather than another undifferentiated list.

Keep the decision tied to the sales process

Timing matters: a quotation can be open for a legitimate business reason. Lead-time context and follow-up rules help avoid reducing the problem to “old quotation equals urgent.” A useful automation should preserve that context when it hands work to a person.

My work focused on the decision workflow and its integration with business automation. This public description does not specify a customer-send approval policy or expose internal customer records.

Repeatable follow-up

The project gives sales recovery a scheduled, consistent workflow: detect an opportunity, prioritize it, and connect it to an action. Exact recovered revenue and conversion figures remain confidential, so I do not use them as public performance claims.
