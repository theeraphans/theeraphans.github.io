Reports should not each rebuild the same business logic

When reporting and automation draw from operational data independently, the same transformations get repeated in different places. That makes it harder to maintain a consistent view of the business and harder to trace where a number came from.

At MiSUMi, I worked on a data foundation that gives downstream reporting and workflows reusable warehouse models. The work is less visible than an AI interface, but it supports the systems that depend on reliable business data.

From operational sources to shared models

Airflow schedules extraction and loading. SQL and dbt transform the loaded data into warehouse models, and Power BI and automation consume those shared models. Docker packages the pipeline environment so its dependencies are not left to individual machines.

{{architecture}}

The distinction between a pipeline and a model is useful. A pipeline moves and prepares data on a schedule. A model expresses reusable business logic. A report should consume that logic, not quietly implement a competing version of it.

Build the foundation behind the dashboard

My contribution covered data pipelines, transformation models, and their connection to reporting and business automation. Organizing those responsibilities creates a clearer path from a report back to its sources and transformations.

This work sits alongside inquiry intelligence and sales recovery. Those workflows need business data; the data platform gives reporting and automation a common place to get it.

What this public architecture shows
