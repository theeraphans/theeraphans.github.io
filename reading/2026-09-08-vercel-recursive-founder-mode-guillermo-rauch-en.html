Vercel's Recursive Founder Mode

Guillermo Rauch's AI-era playbook is not simply “work harder” or “let agents code.” It is a system for distributing founder-like ownership, encoding organizational taste, and building one continuously improving machine that helps the company build everything else.

Executive summary

The company becomes the factory that builds factories

Rauch argues that classic founder mode has a scaling limit: one founder cannot personally drive every detail across a multi-product company serving millions of customers. Vercel's answer is “recursive founder mode.” It recruits people with entrepreneurial judgment, gives them end-to-end ownership of a product, and lets them operate almost like CEOs inside a common platform.

AI adds a second recursive layer. Product leaders no longer improve only an output; they improve the system that repeatedly produces outputs. Skills capture institutional know-how. Specialized agents perform work in functions such as sales, data, design review, and security. A company-wide agent named V gives employees one front door to these capabilities and learns from their use.

PeopleFounder-like owners

Small, autonomous leaders own product, customer understanding, communication, and business outcomes.

PlatformA high shared floor

Infrastructure, design primitives, distribution, and company values make quality easier to reproduce.

AgentsCompounding machinery

Skills and agents convert repeated judgment into systems that improve the next cycle of work.

The larger claim is that AI changes the unit of professional value. Writing code, making a slide deck, or querying data becomes cheaper. What remains scarce is choosing what deserves to exist, connecting it to customers, allocating tokens and capital, building trustworthy systems, and supplying human taste and care.

The central idea

AI does not remove management or product judgment. It pushes both upward: from supervising individual tasks to designing the people, interfaces, constraints, feedback loops, and economics of an autonomous production system.

Organization · 02:01

What recursive founder mode actually means

As Vercel expanded from frontend hosting into backends, agents, and the wider software-development lifecycle, Rauch needed multiple centers of founder-level energy. He points to leaders of products such as the AI SDK and Fluid Compute: people with a strong view of the market who can cross engineering, product, design, marketing, and customer communication.

The product owner receives a pool of autonomy, but not an isolated kingdom. Company leadership still sets values, north stars, and operating constraints. The product is built on Vercel's shared infrastructure and benefits from its customer base and distribution. This combination lets entrepreneurial people pursue an idea with more leverage than a standalone startup might have.

OwnThe whole outcome

The internal founder is responsible for what is built, how it is explained, whether customers adopt it, and whether it contributes to the business.

SharePlatform and standards

Teams reuse deployment infrastructure, UI primitives, company values, distribution, and growing agent capabilities rather than rebuilding a company around every product.

MeasureSpeed and customer value

Autonomy is not freedom from accountability. The recurring tests are shipping cadence, quality, customer response, retention, expansion, and business impact.

This model also answers a talent question. Seed funding may be plentiful, but some products work better inside a broad platform. An internal founder can retain creative ownership while receiving a mature delivery channel, adjacent services, and an existing customer relationship.

Operating system · 07:28 and 23:41

Skills turn taste into a scalable quality system

Rauch treats quality as an organizational system rather than the CEO's personal approval queue. Vercel published web-development know-how through skills.sh so external and internal agents can produce better Next.js and React work. The strategic bet is that giving away knowledge increases trust in the broader platform.

Internally, the same mechanism can encode how Vercel reviews user interfaces, communicates launches, researches customers, or handles common engineering decisions. Rauch rejects the idea that speed and taste must oppose each other. Raw model output is often generic, but that is a failure to provide standards, context, review, and reusable craft knowledge.

01Observe expert work
02Extract principles
03Encode a skill
04Review agent output
05Feed lessons back

Shared component systems play a similar role. Good buttons, sidebars, interaction patterns, and AI-specific components raise the minimum quality of every team. The point is not to automate taste completely; it is to move expert judgment earlier into the platform and make routine violations easier to catch.

Talent · 10:14

Work-prompt interviews test how candidates build

Vercel asks candidates to create an artifact during the interview process. The assignment varies by role, but the evaluation extends beyond whether the artifact works. Interviewers look at how candidates use AI, explain decisions, notice details, explore useful side paths, and identify missing platform capabilities.

A strong candidate may return with a polished result and a precise account of where an hour was wasted. That observation is evidence of product thinking: the candidate did the work, diagnosed friction, and arrived with a view of what the company should improve.

The approach also applies outside engineering. Rauch expects marketers to build agents and artifacts directly instead of depending on a ticket queue for every creative or operational task. The desired employee is a problem owner who can assemble the means of production, not a narrow specialist who only forwards requirements.

EvidenceArtifact over credentials

Recent proof of building reveals judgment, fluency, and follow-through more directly than a resume alone.

ProcessReasoning over polish alone

The interesting signal includes choices, discoveries, limitations, and the candidate's ability to teach the organization.

Future fitTrajectory over static expertise

In a rapidly changing tool environment, learning speed and willingness to revise assumptions matter greatly.

RiskFairness still needs design

Take-home work can favor candidates with more time or tooling. Clear scope, compensation where appropriate, and consistent evaluation remain necessary.

Throughput · 14:33

Few choke points, fast feedback, explicit security stops

Rauch says he remains involved in every hire at a company approaching 800 people, but agents compress candidate histories, interview progress, work samples, and questions so he can review asynchronously. The pattern illustrates his preferred use of AI: preserve consequential judgment while reducing the cost of gathering context.

Vercel's “iterative greatness” value favors shipping and improving over waiting indefinitely for unanimous approval. Most leaders cannot simply block a launch; they must own a veto and explain it. Security is the important exception and can stop production. Rauch describes the company as deliberately engineering only a small number of carefully placed choke points.

The company studies operational throughput such as changelog frequency and deployment feedback time. This is why “everything is code” matters in the interview: documents, spreadsheets, hiring loops, support workflows, and release processes can all be treated as systems with inputs, tool calls, constraints, observability, and opportunities for automation.

Engineering · 26:43

The new meta-skill is building the software factory

Rauch compares resistance to coding agents with earlier resistance to JavaScript, the web, mobile, and cloud computing. In each transition, people who tied their identity to the old implementation layer risked missing the new platform. His best engineers adopted AI tools early and developed a more precise understanding of their strengths and failure modes.

Simple prompting is only the first rung. The higher-value skill is designing the factory that generates, tests, reviews, secures, deploys, and learns from software. An issue in the AI SDK, for example, can become structured work that an agent investigates, implements, typifies, validates, and prepares for release as underlying model capabilities change.

01Capture issue or opportunity
02Supply context and skills
03Generate and test
04Deploy and observe
05Update the factory

This pushes engineering toward the “product engineer”: someone who understands customers, product economics, retention, communication, and technical systems together. The organization becomes flatter because a small, high-agency team can own more of the complete loop.

Career signal · 32:21

Building is the baseline; adoption is the differentiator

When Rauch was young, publishing useful open-source code was rare enough to signal exceptional ability. AI lowers that barrier. A young builder now differentiates through the distance traveled after generation: finding a non-obvious problem, communicating why it matters, attracting users, learning from them, operating economically, and pushing models beyond standard output.

He uses independent makers as examples of people who combine software, storytelling, landing-page design, distribution, and revenue analysis. Open source is also becoming more business-like: naming, documentation, identity, community, and real customer stories increasingly matter alongside code quality.

Agent architecture · 35:47

V gives the whole company one front door

Giving every employee a generic AI subscription widened the gap between expert users and everyone else. Some people accumulated prompt habits, tools, and skills while others barely changed their work. Vercel's response was to centralize access through its internal agent, V, built on an open framework called EVE.

V is not presented as one giant model that knows everything. It is a stable interface over specialized agents, applications, company data, capabilities, and skills, maintained by a dedicated team. Employees use the same entry point for business questions, customer context, research, data analysis, and automation requests.

EmployeeAsk V once
RouteInterpret intent and policy
DelegateCall specialist agents
ActUse tools and company data
LearnImprove shared capability

The data organization is a concrete example. Instead of staffing a ticket queue where analysts answer repeated questions, it builds the machinery that lets employees ask V. Access policy is part of the system, time to first token replaces a human SLA for many requests, and the same agent can combine business facts into useful observations.

Executives remain necessary in this account, but their work changes. They design agents, encode operating rituals, set policy, decide where humans remain accountable, and invest in shared capability. Specialist agents can stay invisible to most employees because V handles discovery and routing.

Software strategy · 38:47

Agent interoperability becomes a purchasing criterion

Traditional software quality still matters, but it becomes table stakes. Rauch now evaluates vendors by how reliably they interoperate with the company's agent. Merely publishing an MCP server is insufficient if a normal workflow requires thousands of tool calls, fails unpredictably, exposes a poor permission model, or cannot handle agent-generated load.

This creates two pressures. First, software must offer efficient machine interfaces such as robust APIs, CLIs, or agent protocols. Second, infrastructure must absorb bursty automation. A workload can grow by orders of magnitude when agents perform tasks continuously and in parallel, exposing capacity problems that were hidden under human request rates.

InterfaceMachine usable

Tools need compact schemas, composable operations, predictable errors, and permission-aware access rather than a checkbox integration.

ReliabilityAgent scale

Infrastructure must tolerate parallelism and rapid retries without turning automation into a queue or outage amplifier.

EconomicsToken-aware operation

A technically successful integration can still be unusable if each outcome consumes too many calls, tokens, or human corrections.

Leadership · 42:11

AI changes the CEO's attention allocation

Rauch believes leaders must personally use the new tools because they cannot understand a platform shift only through delegated reports. He uses command-line agents for personal research and V in Slack for company work, often launching long-running research tasks asynchronously.

A question about the AI Gateway signup journey once would have required scheduling a product review, assigning screenshots, and waiting for a presentation. Now an agent can perform the journey and report friction. Rauch says a direct message to a colleague increasingly means the agent could not finish, which makes human attention more deliberate.

He does not frame the future as human versus agent. A more likely pattern is human, colleague, and agent sharing a conversation with appropriate consent and privacy. The agent supplies context or takes actions; the people retain relationship, accountability, and judgment.

Benchmark models instead of relying on reputation

Vercel's security work illustrates why model choice must be empirical and continuously revisited. Different models vary in vulnerability discovery, cost, and latency. A stronger model may be too expensive for broad scanning, while a slightly weaker open model may produce better economics. Routing should therefore depend on task risk, benchmark evidence, and budget.

Rauch expects token spending to become a material operating cost. Companies will need to decide how much intelligence each workflow deserves, which model tier to use, and when expected value justifies expensive reasoning. Agent architecture therefore becomes part of the P&L, not merely an IT choice.

Product and infrastructure · 54:40

Start the company with its agent; start the user with v0

If Rauch founded a company today, he would establish its internal agent at the beginning, alongside or even before the conventional website and cloud stack. The agent would accumulate company knowledge, personality, tools, policies, and operational capability as the organization grows.

v0 applies the same “raise the floor” logic to customers. Its purpose is not simply to generate code, but to make the first useful, Vercel-quality artifact arrive faster. It packages platform knowledge and removes setup friction, then hands the project into the larger application cloud.

Vercel itself is adapting to agents as users. The web era needed CDNs for pages; Rauch describes the AI Gateway as analogous infrastructure for tokens, with secure sandboxes and agent frameworks as additional primitives. The user may not choose each service manually because the building agent can select and configure its own stack.

This also changes design work. Human-facing screens remain important, but companies now need to design APIs and CLIs with the same care once reserved for visual interfaces. An agent wants direct, structured access to foundational capabilities, not a graphical workflow built around clicks.

Markets · 1:03:48

Easy creation moves the moat toward quality and operations

Rauch does not predict the end of software. He expects a recalibration. Bespoke tools become easier for companies to build around their own workflows, which weakens some narrow SaaS products. At the same time, infrastructure and broad platforms gain importance because security, global operation, reliability, distribution, and integration cannot be produced safely by a casual generation step.

AI may also revive small businesses and independent software. A small team can produce what once needed a large venture-backed organization, while platforms provide payments, commerce, deployment, and distribution. The opportunity may be profitable without fitting the old venture-scale template.

He is optimistic that cheaper creation will make the web more expressive. Industrial content systems optimized for scale pushed experiences toward standardized pages. Generative tools can make interactive, highly specific experiences affordable again, allowing artists and domain experts to shape software directly.

Stronger moatInfrastructure and trust

Operational history, security practice, scale, and dependable delivery remain difficult to imitate.

Weaker moatRigid narrow workflows

A customer may replace several off-the-shelf tools with one platform and custom agents built around proprietary processes.

New opportunitySmall differentiated businesses

Low production cost supports specialized software, creative experiences, and profitable teams that do not need enormous headcount.

Scarce resourceAttention and distribution

When everyone can build, customer trust, storytelling, adoption, and sustained service become more valuable.

Human boundary

Keep values, relationships, and capital allocation human

Rauch expects models to solve more bugs, performance problems, and security issues as token budgets grow. What should remain under human control is the hierarchy of purpose: which problems deserve resources, what values shape the product, what aesthetic and social outcomes are acceptable, and who is accountable.

He also draws a personal boundary around empathy and connection. AI can research and review his writing, but he does not want it to replace his authentic communication or act as a friend. In his view, human-to-human contact becomes more valuable as generated content becomes abundant.

Inner game · 1:11:24

The leader is also a factory that requires maintenance

Moving from programmer to CEO required Rauch to become more full stack: product, infrastructure, negotiation, recruiting, culture, communication, and management. AI amplifies this expectation because leaders can now cross functional boundaries more easily. Remaining inside one familiar specialty becomes a strategic risk.

He describes personal energy as capacity that can be trained through routines, exercise, sleep, diet, and reflection. He tracks indicators, including an app he built to count daily keystrokes, but accepts that performance has cycles. Low periods become data to observe and improve rather than proof that the mission has ended.

Management scale comes from values and example, not only one-to-one meetings. Rauch still joins customer-support rotations to stay close to actual pain and signal that customer contact matters. He treats most strategic mistakes as reversible: regain focus, endure the correction period, and keep moving.

His closing advice emphasizes sustained discipline and a long time horizon. The interview's rhetoric about extreme hours is intentionally intense, but the more transferable principle is consistency: skill compounds when life is deliberately organized around learning, making, recovery, and repeated contact with reality.

Critical assessment

A compelling system with important tensions

StrongOne front door reduces adoption fragmentation.

A shared agent can centralize permissions, routing, measurement, and reusable knowledge while hiding specialist complexity.

RiskOne front door can become one point of control.

V needs clear provenance, appeal paths, audit logs, and graceful escape hatches when routing or policy is wrong.

StrongSkills can preserve institutional learning.

They are useful when treated as versioned operational assets with owners, tests, usage evidence, and retirement criteria.

RiskEncoded taste can freeze today's assumptions.

Automated review must allow principled exceptions and update from diverse customer outcomes rather than merely reproducing executive preference.

StrongAutonomous product ownership fits AI leverage.

People who own customer and business outcomes can use agents across functional boundaries without waiting on long queues.

RiskExtreme ownership can hide unsustainable load.

High agency should not erase specialization, team health, compliance, or the value of people whose best work is collaborative rather than founder-like.

My conclusion

Recursive founder mode is most useful as an architecture, not a slogan about heroic effort. Its durable pieces are distributed ownership, a strong shared platform, one governed agent interface, reusable skills, explicit security gates, measurable feedback, and humans who remain accountable for purpose and trade-offs.

Takeaways

Twelve ideas worth keeping

  1. Founder mode scales only when ownership can recur inside smaller product units.
  2. Autonomy works best on top of shared infrastructure, values, distribution, and quality primitives.
  3. AI skills are a mechanism for transferring institutional knowledge into repeatable work.
  4. Work-sample interviews should examine decisions, discoveries, and learning, not just the final artifact.
  5. The emerging engineering meta-skill is designing the system that repeatedly produces software.
  6. Building is cheaper, so communication, customers, economics, and follow-through carry more signal.
  7. A company-wide agent can raise the floor when it routes to specialists behind one stable interface.
  8. Agent-ready software needs efficient tools, reliable infrastructure, clear permissions, and manageable call cost.
  9. Executives should use agents directly enough to understand what has become possible.
  10. Token allocation will become an operating and financial discipline.
  11. Infrastructure, trust, distribution, and operational rigor become stronger moats as code becomes abundant.
  12. Humans must retain responsibility for purpose, values, relationships, and consequential resource allocation.
