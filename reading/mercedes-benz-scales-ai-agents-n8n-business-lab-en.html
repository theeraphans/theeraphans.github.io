Core Thesis

Mercedes-Benz is treating AI agents as enterprise infrastructure, not isolated experiments.

The speakers describe an AI ecosystem run from an IT center of competence that supports Mercedes-Benz use cases across vehicles, enterprise processes, production, shop-floor management, after-sales, and software engineering. The goal is to expose models, agents, services, and automation tooling in one governed place.

The important shift is scale. The company is not only letting individual teams try AI tools. It is building a platform where models pass intake, agents can be registered and discovered, business users can build workflows, and more technical teams can still create tailored solutions. n8n enters as a low-code/no-code automation layer inside that broader platform.

The pattern is useful for any large company: create a central AI ecosystem, then let different user groups consume, shape, or build on top of it with the right level of control.
AI Ecosystem

The platform combines a model garden, an agent garden, playgrounds, and reusable AI services.

The Mercedes-Benz AI ecosystem is described as the enterprise layer for AI practitioners worldwide. It provides access to governed models, exposes agents, connects to multi-cloud strategy, and offers playgrounds for software developers and business users to try approved capabilities.

The model garden already exposes more than 40 models. The speakers describe a model intake process where demanded models can be integrated into internal lifecycle and made available to customers. A process that historically took months is described as being automated down to minutes for suitable models.

Adoption Layers

The ecosystem separates takers, shapers, and builders.

The talk describes three user groups. Takers are broad employees who receive productivity tools such as Copilot and learn that AI is part of daily work. Shapers are low-code/no-code users who can automate processes and create agents. Builders are software-engineering teams creating tailored solutions.

This distinction matters because one platform cannot assume every user is a developer. Mercedes-Benz wants white-collar workers to experience productivity gains, business colleagues to automate repeated processes, and builders to connect those capabilities into enterprise software delivery and compliance checks.

Why n8n

n8n is chosen for workflow automation, self-hosting, integrations, and extensibility.

The system architect explains that Mercedes-Benz evaluated several vendors and chose n8n for the workflow automation editor, reduced lock-in, extensive integrations, self-hosting, and the ability to extend the product with internal Mercedes-Benz nodes.

The internal context is crucial. Mercedes-Benz has internal systems such as identity and access management, plus an internal LLM gateway that controls access to language models. n8n can connect to that gateway and can be extended so enterprise workflows use internal systems through safer, packaged nodes instead of ad hoc integrations.

Deployment Architecture

The enterprise setup isolates n8n instances per use case on Kubernetes.

Mercedes-Benz runs n8n as an internal platform on a large Kubernetes cluster operated by a central team. The talk describes separate n8n instances per use case. This keeps data separation and resilience at the platform level.

The example given is simple and important: HR data should not be mixed with car data. Separate instances help prevent unwanted data combination and reduce blast radius. If one workflow or system breaks badly, it should not break the entire Mercedes-Benz n8n setup. At the time of the talk, the setup was described as running more than 30 instances.

Agent Orchestration

n8n fills a gap for orchestrating agents across departments and business processes.

Mercedes-Benz sees a gap around orchestration: multiple agents may already exist in an agent garden, but a business process often needs a higher-level orchestrator that combines them into a single workflow. The talk gives a mock example of an orchestrator connecting pre-sales, sales, and after-sales agents.

This maps cleanly to agentic architecture. The agent garden gives discoverability and governance. n8n gives business users and platform teams a way to compose workflows. A2A and MCP-style interoperability can let agents and tools connect across business chains, while security and management stay centralized.

Rollout

The rollout combines enterprise licensing, MVP, support, workshops, and hackathon adoption.

The n8n enterprise contract was signed in January, followed by ramp-up and an extended MVP phase. The team communicated the rollout, provided first-level support, and ran workshops so employees could learn the tool hands-on.

A major adoption moment was an internal Mercedes-Benz hackathon announced to roughly 90,000 white-collar workers. Teams across China, India, the US, and Germany tested n8n over several days. Hundreds of use-case ideas were planned and executed, and the team received positive early feedback.

Platform Takeaways

What this means for your agentic AI platform.

The talk is a strong enterprise reference because it separates the platform layers: model governance, agent discovery, low-code workflow composition, internal tool wrappers, multi-cloud access, training, and adoption programs. That is exactly the kind of structure needed if BA-side users will assemble multi-agent systems safely.

  • Create a model garden: approved models, provider routing, intake process, compliance metadata, and subscription model.
  • Create an agent garden: registry, owner, purpose, input/output contracts, allowed tools, security status, and reuse path.
  • Make n8n-style orchestration a shaper layer: business users can connect events, agents, tools, and approvals without full custom code.
  • Wrap internal systems as nodes/tools: IAM, CRM, ticketing, product data, knowledge bases, and LLM gateway access should be packaged, not improvised.
  • Isolate by use case or tenant: sensitive domains need separate runtime boundaries and blast-radius control.
  • Use hackathons as discovery: capture workflows, templates, missing connectors, training needs, and candidate reusable agents.
  • Publish useful workflows back to the garden: successful automations should become discoverable platform assets.
