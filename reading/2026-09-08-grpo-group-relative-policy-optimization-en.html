The problem

What was missing before this work

PPO-style RLHF uses a value model to estimate advantages, adding memory and training complexity comparable to another large network. Mathematical reasoning offers outcome rewards that can score sampled answers directly.

DeepSeekMath sought an efficient reinforcement-learning method that could improve reasoning while avoiding a separate critic.

Core idea

The paper in one idea

For each prompt, sample a group of responses from the current or old policy. Score each response, then normalize rewards within the group to create relative advantages. Optimize a clipped policy objective with a KL regularizer.

Each answer is judged against its siblings rather than an absolute learned value baseline. This makes group diversity and reward quality central.

advantage_i = (reward_i − mean(group rewards)) / std(group rewards)

Mechanism

How the method works

Sample G completions for one problem. Apply rule-based or learned rewards. Compute group mean and standard deviation, assign normalized advantages, and update token probabilities with a PPO-like clipped ratio.

Repeat with fresh groups as the policy improves. A reference model or analytic KL term limits destructive drift.

Technical deep dive

The machinery that matters

Removing the critic reduces memory, but sampling several long completions increases inference cost. When all group rewards are equal, normalized advantage carries little information; near-zero variance needs stable handling.

Outcome rewards can reinforce correct final answers while leaving flawed hidden reasoning unexamined. Process rewards offer denser signals but require more reliable labels.

Evidence

What the experiments established

DeepSeekMath reported large gains on mathematical benchmarks after continued pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and GRPO. The full result cannot be attributed to GRPO alone because data and training stages interact.

Later systems expanded GRPO’s visibility, but those results are subsequent evidence and should not be retroactively assigned to the original paper.

Why it mattered

How it changed the field

GRPO became a major method for reasoning-model post-training because it combines online exploration, verifiable rewards, and lower critic memory.

It shifted infrastructure toward high-throughput generation, grouped rollout storage, reward sandboxes, and careful policy-version control.

Engineering use

What an AI engineer should do with it

Use deterministic verifiers where possible and defend them against formatting hacks. Record prompt, policy checkpoint, samples, reward components, and group statistics for every rollout.

Track diversity and reward saturation. If all samples converge to one pattern, group-relative learning weakens and can amplify brittle strategies.

Limits and later corrections

Where the result stops

Relative reward does not tell whether an entire group is good; it only ranks within the group. Weak or hackable verifiers can optimize incorrect behavior confidently.

Online sampling is expensive, and clipping/KL choices remain sensitive. Mathematical correctness provides unusually clean rewards that many business domains lack.

Connections

Where this paper sits in the learning path

GRPO contrasts with offline DPO and ORPO. Chain-of-Thought supplies reasoning traces, while Test-Time Scaling uses extra inference rather than weight updates to select better answers.

Takeaways

What to remember

  • GRPO replaces the learned critic with group-relative reward baselines.
  • Several responses must be sampled for each prompt.
  • Verifiable rewards make the method especially useful for math and code.
  • No critic does not mean low total compute.
  • Equal or hacked rewards provide weak learning signal.
  • Attribute DeepSeekMath gains to the full pipeline, not one objective.
