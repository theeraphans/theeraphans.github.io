Building an AI Second Brain for Investing

A practical walkthrough of turning scattered filings, PDFs, videos, articles, and research into a local LLM Wiki: source-grounded notes that both people and coding agents can read, connect, search, and extend.

Mick · หุ้นการบ้าน AI Battle in Finance · QuantCorner 47m 35s Summary run September 7, 2026 · 13:08 ICT

This is an English editorial paraphrase of the complete Thai-language session. It explains a research workflow, not an investment recommendation. Tool behavior and financial facts should be independently verified before use.

Executive summary

Do not make the model remember everything; give it a library

Investors work across earnings calls, annual reports, MD&A sections, spreadsheets, news, research notes, interviews, and videos. Copying all of that into one chat produces an expensive and fragile conversation. The session proposes a different architecture: keep durable knowledge in plain local files, organize it with lightweight metadata, and let an agent retrieve or transform only the material needed for the current task.

The speaker calls this an LLM Wiki: a personal Wikipedia in which notes link to concepts, companies, people, industries, and original sources. Obsidian supplies the local, human-readable workspace. Markdown keeps content compact and portable. YAML front matter gives each note structured fields such as type, tags, date, and source. Coding agents handle the tedious work of ingesting documents, extracting concepts, checking facts, linking entities, saving assets, and updating indexes.

The system is valuable only if it improves thinking. The closing warning is the most important: a Second Brain should outsource storage and clerical organization, not the investor's First Brain. AI can help gather and structure evidence; the human still needs to question assumptions, compare sources, understand uncertainty, and own the decision.

Central thesis

A durable AI knowledge system separates raw evidence from derived knowledge, preserves traceability back to the source, and uses agents to maintain structure—while keeping analysis and investment judgment under human control.

Foundation

Why long chats are a weak knowledge base

Stateless sessionsContinuity must be supplied

A model does not automatically retain a reliable memory across independent sessions. Every task needs enough context to understand the user, the objective, and the relevant evidence.

Context windowThe working desk is finite

The speaker compares the context window to a desk. Documents, user messages, model responses, and tool results all compete for space. More context is not always better; irrelevant or repetitive material can distract the model and raise cost.

Context rotSignal weakens as the session grows

Long-running conversations may lose early constraints, bury important evidence, or produce less consistent answers. The practical advice is to summarize completed work, save durable results externally, and begin a clean session for the next bounded task.

CompactionA continuation is not perfect memory

Applications can compress prior conversation to keep a session alive, but compression inevitably selects and abstracts. Critical facts and source references belong in persistent notes rather than only inside conversational history.

The presentation uses approximate context and token figures to build intuition. Exact limits, caching behavior, and pricing vary by model and product; the lasting principle is to keep the active context purposeful and move durable knowledge into an external system.

Human knowledge

A Second Brain stores; the First Brain reasons

Drawing on Tiago Forte's Second Brain concept, the session argues that human cognition is better used for interpretation than for memorizing every document. A digital system can preserve information, connect ideas, and make prior work retrievable. Yet traditional note systems often impose their own burden: users spend time learning formats, choosing folders, applying tags, and maintaining links.

The proposed use of AI is narrow but powerful. Let the agent perform repetitive knowledge maintenance—classification, formatting, extraction, and linking—so the investor spends more time evaluating the business and less time grooming a database.

LayerBest responsibilityFailure to avoid
First BrainQuestion, interpret, compare, model uncertainty, decideAccepting a generated conclusion without understanding its evidence
Second BrainStore, connect, retrieve, preserve provenanceBecoming an elaborate archive that is never used
AI agentIngest, transform, verify, link, index, answer from sourcesInventing facts, overwriting evidence, or acting without review

Data model

Why Obsidian, Markdown, and YAML fit the job

Local filesPortable ownership

An Obsidian vault is a folder on the user's machine. Notes are not trapped in a proprietary database, and ordinary file tools or agents can inspect them. Cloud sync can be added separately when desired.

MarkdownReadable by human and machine

Headings, lists, checkboxes, tables, emphasis, and links are represented with little markup. The same file remains pleasant to read in Obsidian and efficient for an LLM to parse.

YAML front matterPredictable metadata

Fields at the top of a note can identify title, type, company, ticker, tags, source URL, creation date, period, and review status. A schema makes those fields consistent enough for filtering and automation.

Wiki linksConnected concepts

A company note can link to its CEO, industry, competitors, products, and financial concepts. Obsidian visualizes those relationships as a graph, but the valuable part is navigability and reuse—not the visual spectacle itself.

LLM-Wiki/ ├── raw/ Original transcripts, filings, PDFs and captured pages ├── wiki/ Concepts, companies, people, industries and relationships ├── logs/ What each ingestion or research run changed ├── schema/ Required fields and writing rules for each note type ├── index/ Searchable views of the knowledge base ├── assets/ Extracted images, charts and supporting files └── agents/ Project instructions, skills and specialized workers

The folder boundaries separate evidence from interpretation. A raw source should remain available even when the derived concept note is later rewritten. That distinction is essential in financial research, where conclusions change but the historical record should not.

Ingestion workflow

From source material to linked, reviewable knowledge

01Capture source
02Store raw
03Extract facts
04Cross-check
05Create concepts
06Link & index
07Human review
CaptureAccept many source types

The demo feeds the system YouTube links, MD&A PDFs, articles, and company research. The speaker also mentions earnings calls, Excel files, analyst material, and recorded management conversations converted to text.

Raw preservationKeep an audit trail

The source is written into the raw area before summarization. Video becomes a timed transcript; documents retain extracted tables or sections; useful visual material can be saved to assets.

ResearchVerify names, numbers, and context

A research worker examines whether extracted information is plausible. Another agent may open the source link or retrieve supporting information. The workflow is intended to reduce transcription errors and ambiguous figures before they become durable notes.

Knowledge writingSeparate entities and concepts

Derived notes describe entities such as a company, executive, or media channel and concepts such as an industry, strategy, or financial mechanism. Each note follows the schema and links back to sources and related notes.

Index and logMake maintenance observable

The index shows what exists and what was created recently. Logs record which workflow ran and what it changed, helping the user understand agent activity rather than treating the vault as mysteriously self-updating.

Agent organization

The project is governed by files, skills, and specialized workers

A top-level project instruction file—shown as agent-oriented Markdown files for different coding assistants—tells a newly opened agent what the repository is, how notes are structured, and which rules apply. Skills expose repeatable commands such as onboarding, ingestion, and research. Specialized sub-agents divide responsibilities such as collecting a source, checking it, writing concepts, and reviewing numbers.

The onboarding skill interviews the user about the intended knowledge base and explains unfamiliar project concepts. The ingest skill accepts a source and runs the transformation pipeline. The research skill starts from a company or topic when the user does not already have a source.

The live demonstration runs different coding-agent products against the same starter structure. The point is portability: because the core state is a folder of documented text files, the knowledge base is not completely coupled to one model vendor or chat interface.

Design principle

Prompts should not be the only architecture. Durable instructions, typed metadata, bounded skills, source-preserving workflows, review steps, and logs make the system understandable across sessions and agent products.

Live demonstration

What the audience sees the system produce

The speaker opens the starter project as an Obsidian vault, starts a coding agent in the project directory, and invokes ingestion on several sources. Examples include a trading-related YouTube video, an MD&A document for Delta, a topic relating to Webull, and an options video. Several agent products run in parallel to show that the file-oriented workflow can be shared.

Completed runs populate the raw folder with source text, create entity and concept notes, record source links, extract selected images, and update the index. A reviewer checks important numbers before the result is presented as a finished Markdown note. The user can then navigate links in Obsidian or ask natural-language questions through an embedded agent sidebar.

The demo also shows a simpler intake route. A user may paste article text directly into the agent or use an Obsidian web clipper to save a webpage into the raw folder. Automation is helpful, but capture remains useful even when a particular parser or source connector fails.

For financial research

Structure each claim so it can be challenged later

ProvenanceAttach claim to source and date

A revenue figure without reporting period, currency, filing page, publication date, and source type is not durable knowledge. Preserve enough context to reproduce the claim.

Fact vs. interpretationDo not blend evidence and thesis

Keep reported figures, management statements, analyst assumptions, and the investor's own inference distinguishable. Each deserves a different confidence level.

Temporal validityKnowledge expires

Management, guidance, product mix, regulation, and valuation change. Notes need an as-of date and a review status so retrieval does not present stale information as current truth.

ContradictionPreserve disagreement

A useful Wiki should not collapse conflicting sources into one confident summary. Link bullish and bearish evidence, record uncertainty, and surface what would falsify the thesis.

Decision recordTrack what changed the view

Record the thesis, assumptions, observed evidence, rejected alternatives, and later outcome. This turns the archive into a learning system rather than a collection of polished summaries.

Critical assessment

The architecture is strong; automation needs firmer controls

01Verification cannot be model-only

A second agent may repeat the first agent's mistake. Material figures should be checked against primary filings, official transcripts, exchange disclosures, or reproducible calculations.

02Autonomous permissions are risky

The demo uses modes that let agents act without asking repeatedly. That is convenient in a disposable starter vault, but a production system should restrict file paths, network destinations, commands, spending, and irreversible changes.

03Graph density is not insight

A beautiful network can reward excessive linking. Connections should answer a research question or support retrieval; otherwise they become visual clutter and maintenance cost.

04Summaries can erase nuance

MD&A language, accounting footnotes, and executive interviews often contain qualification and uncertainty. Derived notes need direct source anchors so readers can inspect the original context.

05Private knowledge needs governance

Local storage improves ownership but does not guarantee privacy once cloud models or external tools read the files. Secrets, licensed research, personal recordings, and material non-public information require access controls and explicit data-handling policy.

My assessment

The session gets the most important design choice right: make the filesystem the durable memory and the model a replaceable worker. Its strongest implementation would add typed provenance, freshness checks, primary-source verification, permission boundaries, and a mandatory human review state for investment claims.

Takeaways

What to keep from the session

  1. Keep active model context small and move durable knowledge into an external, inspectable store.
  2. Use local Markdown files when portability, human readability, and agent access matter.
  3. Define a schema before automating ingestion so notes remain consistent across sources and agents.
  4. Preserve raw evidence separately from concepts, entities, and summaries.
  5. Give ingestion, research, writing, review, and indexing separate responsibilities.
  6. For finance, track source, reporting period, as-of date, confidence, contradictions, and reviewer status.
  7. Use the Second Brain to reduce clerical work—not to outsource skepticism or investment judgment.
