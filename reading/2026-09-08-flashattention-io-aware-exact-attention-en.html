The problem

What was missing before this work

Standard attention forms an N-by-N score matrix, writes it to GPU high-bandwidth memory, applies softmax, and reads it again. Those transfers dominate runtime before arithmetic capacity is exhausted.

Approximate attention reduces computation but may change outputs. The paper asks whether exact attention can move far fewer bytes.

Core idea

The paper in one idea

Process query and key/value blocks that fit in fast on-chip SRAM. Compute partial scores and update a numerically stable online softmax plus output accumulator, never storing the complete attention matrix in HBM.

Backward recomputes score tiles from saved normalization statistics, trading cheap arithmetic for expensive memory traffic.

Tile Q,K,V; maintain running row max m and normalization ℓ for online softmax.

Mechanism

How the method works

Load a K/V tile, iterate over Q tiles, compute block scores, apply masks, update running maximum and denominator, and rescale the prior output accumulator before adding the new block.

Write only the final output and small softmax statistics. During backward, reconstruct each tile and accumulate gradients.

Technical deep dive

The machinery that matters

Online softmax combines block statistics exactly: when a new block has a larger max, old sums and outputs are rescaled into the new exponent frame. This preserves numerical stability.

IO complexity depends on SRAM size and tiling. Causal masking skips useless regions, and kernel fusion avoids launches and intermediate buffers.

Evidence

What the experiments established

The paper reported substantial wall-clock speedups over PyTorch attention and much lower memory, enabling longer sequences on tested GPUs. End-to-end Transformer training also accelerated.

Speedups vary with GPU, dtype, head dimension, sequence length, dropout, and competing kernels. The theoretical IO advantage does not imply one fixed multiplier.

Why it mattered

How it changed the field

FlashAttention made hardware-aware exact algorithms central to LLM engineering and enabled longer contexts without changing model semantics.

It became the foundation for production attention kernels and later improvements in work partitioning, decoding, and new accelerator generations.

Engineering use

What an AI engineer should do with it

Use framework-native fused scaled-dot-product attention when it dispatches to a supported kernel. Confirm masks, dropout, head size, and dtype do not trigger a slow fallback.

Benchmark end to end with realistic sequence distributions. A faster attention kernel may not dominate when feed-forward layers, communication, or data loading are the bottleneck.

Limits and later corrections

Where the result stops

Dense arithmetic remains quadratic in sequence length even though memory is linearized. Very long contexts eventually hit compute and KV-cache limits.

Custom kernels are hardware- and shape-sensitive. Numerical differences from operation order are small but can complicate exact bitwise reproducibility.

Takeaways

What to remember

  • FlashAttention is exact attention with a different IO schedule.
  • SRAM tiling avoids the full HBM score matrix.
  • Online softmax preserves stable normalization across tiles.
  • Backward recomputation saves memory.
  • Quadratic arithmetic remains.
  • Supported shapes and kernels determine actual speed.
