Eligibility, priority, and action in sales recovery

Why quotation matching and follow-up ranking should remain separate decisions.

Theeraphan Sukchok · 9 October 2026 · Engineering research note

On this page

Where this question comes from

This note starts from my Sales Recovery workflow, which connects quotation and order information, business rules, scheduled monitoring, and Power Automate follow-up. Internal matching rules and commercial outcomes are not disclosed. The examples below explain design risks rather than report internal incidents.

This engineering note develops a testable question from my work. It does not report a new experiment or claim a novel algorithm.

Find the gap, then decide what to follow up

The workflow compares quotations with order information to identify opportunities that still need follow-up. It considers quotation value, age, and lead-time context rather than treating every open quotation as equally urgent.

Business rules and heuristic inference guide the recovery decision. Airflow schedules the monitoring process, while Power Automate connects its decisions to tasks, notifications, and email workflows. The sales team receives a prioritized opportunity rather than another undifferentiated list.

Define the unit of follow-up first

A quotation can contain several items, and an order may convert only part of it. Before defining a recovery rule, the workflow needs to state whether it acts on a whole quotation, a quotation line, or a customer-level opportunity.

Imagine a three-line quotation where two lines have become orders. Treating it as untouched overstates the remaining opportunity; treating it as fully converted loses the third line. This fabricated example explains the modeling problem, not the project's confidential matching policy.

A reproducible decision also needs an observation time. An order arriving after yesterday's scheduled run should affect today's decision without rewriting yesterday's explanation. The data window belongs beside the decision record.

Separate eligibility, priority, and action

Eligibility asks whether follow-up is still needed. Priority compares eligible opportunities. Action determines the task or notification to create. Separating these questions lets a team adjust ranking without quietly changing what counts as an open opportunity.

EligibilityIs business still outstanding?Converted quotation selected

The existing workflow uses business rules and heuristic inference. Calling it an agent does not establish that an LLM freely controls each step. Explicit rules provide a place to inspect why an opportunity was selected.

Microsoft's Power Automate guidance covers organizing cloud flows and keeping configuration manageable. It is relevant background for the integration layer. Power Automate coding guidelines.

What I would test next

Create a controlled fixture with unconverted, fully converted, and partly converted quotations. Test eligibility against explicit expected answers before comparing priority rules. Freeze the observation time so later orders do not change the explanation of an earlier decision.

This is a proposed test, not a completed result. A future report needs its fixtures, baseline, and execution evidence.
